01 · Brand Identity

The surgeon who kept the part of medicine everyone else sold off

Two archetypes finished level at 8 points each, and they are not competing for the same job. One is what the brand is. The other is how it is built. Lover leads: the relationship between you and a patient is not the setting for the care, it is the thing being sold. Caregiver sits underneath as machinery — the financing, the foundation, the promise that the model will not become a velvet rope.

Lover · The What
The relationship is the product

A surgeon who has run 600 joint replacements a year inside the volume machine, and is now building the opposite of it on purpose.

  • Your stated purpose, verbatim: “to connect to others.”
  • Your symbol was touching hands. Your character was the kind neighbor.
  • Among your three deepest fears: rejection — the Lover’s fear, word for word.
Caregiver · The How
Service, built into the structure

Level on points, and it shows up where most brands never put it: in the financial architecture rather than the voice.

  • You’d reward the people who “consistently offer empathy and support to everyone around them.”
  • Your brand’s character: “nurturing and compassionate.”
  • Asked about the patient who cannot pay, you held the price and then built financing and a foundation to answer it.
Magician · The Ambition
A model, not just a practice

Third at 5 points, and it decided a tie of its own: asked what would matter in ten years, you chose other surgeons running your model.

  • Among your three strengths: transformative, and innovative.
  • Among your fears: stagnation.
  • You wrote that no practice like this exists in the country. You are not opting out. You are building a template.

At the core, you’re a Lover.

The Lover’s drive is intimacy: devotion, appreciation, being chosen. It is the least expected archetype in orthopaedic surgery and it is exactly right for what you are building. Your purpose answer was “to connect to others,” your symbol was touching hands, and your deepest fear is rejection. In the clarifying session you chose, four times out of five, the answer where the relationship is the point: the panel is capped so the bond can be rare, the patient should leave the first consult struck, and the compliment you want is “I would never go to anyone else.” That is not a service brand. It is a devotion brand.Lean into: the hour itself, the phone that gets answered, being chosen and choosing back.

Caregiver is how it is delivered.

Caregiver tied at 8 and belongs in the delivery rather than the voice, because that is where you actually put it. Asked about a patient who genuinely needs you and cannot pay, you would not take the clean Lover answer. You held the line on the model and immediately described medical financing and a charitable foundation. That is the Caregiver 8 in its correct place: not softening the language, but building the structure that keeps the promise honest. Lean into: the financing plan on the website, the foundation named and dated, access as engineering rather than sentiment.

Why the combination is one-of-a-kind.

The twelve archetypes group into four families by core drive. Connector carries 16 of your 37 points and Structural carries 12 — your two leaders come from different families, and that crossing is the whole brand. Connector archetypes are about the bond; Structural archetypes are about the system that holds it. Most concierge medicine picks one: warmth with no architecture, or architecture with no warmth. You scored both, and you have already built both. Note what is absent: Sage 0 and Hero 0, from a surgeon who trained 40 residents and 13 fellows and leads national advocacy. The expertise is not in question and your instincts know it. The brand should not spend a word arguing for your competence.

The one thing to make unmistakable
Every surgeon in Williamson County can replace a hip. Not one of them can give you the afternoon. You are not selling a better operation. You are selling the only version of it that still comes with a doctor attached.
“The last practice where the surgeon is actually yours.”
The identity in one line: the relationship is the product, the structure protects it, and the whole thing is a model somebody else should copy
One drive sits underneath: Explorer, at 4.“You only get one life, make it count” was your motto, and rugged mountains was your setting. It reads as freedom, and it is worth saying plainly that it is not: asked directly, you said the stallion and the mountains were about vitality and activity. That is the patient after surgery, not you leaving the system. Keep it as an outcome, never as a rebellion.

The guardrail: Lover leads; Caregiver builds. Devotion without the structure becomes a spa, and structure without the devotion becomes a fee schedule. If a line could appear on a med-spa homepage or in an insurance brochure, it is the wrong line. You are neither.

03 · Core Purpose

Put the doctor and the patient back in the same room

Wilco Arthritis Institute exists for the person over fifty who still hikes, still plays, still has places to be, and who is losing that life to a hip or a knee — and who has discovered that the system built to fix it will give them ten minutes with a surgeon they may not meet again until they are already sedated. The technical case is real — fellowship training in joint preservation and reconstruction, six hundred of these a year — but it is not the case that wins, because every practice in the market makes it in nearly the same words and a patient has no way to referee it. What you can prove, and they structurally cannot, is the thing the volume model sold off to survive: a surgeon who knows your name, has your whole problem in his head, and picks up the phone. The operation restores the motion. The relationship is what you are actually charging for, and it is the only part of orthopaedics that nobody in your market is currently selling.

It is the Lover’s purpose — to connect to others, in your own words — delivered the Caregiver’s way: with financing, a foundation, and a structure that means the promise survives contact with someone’s bank account.

06 · Differentiation Strategy

Five ways to pull away

Each pillar targets the open lane, and each is something the brand should actively say rather than imply. Three of the five are things your competitors are structurally unable to copy.

Sell the hour, not the operation.

Every practice in all three camps sells the procedure — techniques, implants, robotic assistance, recovery timelines. Whatever the real differences are between them, a patient cannot audit them, and every competitor is making the claim in nearly the same words. What differs visibly, and can be checked before anyone books anything, is the hour: who is in the room, how long they stay, and whether they are there again afterwards. That is your product and it should be quantified rather than implied. A capped panel is a number. Sixty minutes is a number. Numbers are what make a claim survive a comparison.

In the brandPut the panel cap and the appointment length on the homepage as figures. “A few hundred patients a year,” “a full hour,” “the surgeon you meet is the surgeon who operates.” Lead with those, not with implants and technique — the technical detail belongs in the consultation, where you can actually answer for it.

Put the price on the website.

Not one practice in your named set publishes a price, and it is not cowardice — billing insurance means they genuinely do not know it. Your model is the only one that can, and published pricing does something no adjective achieves: it proves the respect you are claiming. A number on a page tells a fifty-five-year-old you are not going to do anything to them they did not agree to. For a Lover brand whose fear is rejection, transparency is also the mechanism that makes being chosen possible — people cannot choose what they cannot see.

In the brandA real pricing page, itemised, with what is included and what happens if something goes wrong. No “contact us for pricing.” No “investment.” The number, and what it buys.

Say plainly who this is not for.

The instinct with a premium model is to soften the exclusion. Do the opposite. A brand that will not name its limits reads as though it is hoping you will not notice the price. Saying “if you want the fastest route to a covered surgery, we are the wrong practice, and here is who is right” costs you patients who were never going to convert and converts the ones who were, harder. This is the Lover’s selection working in your favour: the panel is small because it is chosen, and being chosen has to run both directions to mean anything.

In the brandA short, unapologetic “is this right for you” section. Name the patient this suits and the patient it does not. Refer the second one somewhere real.

Make the financing and the foundation part of the story, not the fine print.

The obvious attack on a cash practice is that it is care for the wealthy. You have already answered it structurally — medical financing now, a charitable foundation as the goal — and that answer is currently invisible. Left in a footnote it reads defensive. Stated in the open it becomes the proof that this is a reimagined model rather than a price increase, and it is the clearest expression of your Caregiver 8 anywhere in the business.

In the brandFinancing on the pricing page, in the same type size as the price. When the foundation exists, give it a name and a page. Never phrase either as charity for the patient; phrase both as the model working as designed.

Promise motion, not repair.

The category talks about pain: pain scores, pain relief, pain management. You told us what you are actually after — relieve pain, restore motion, improve people’s activity levels — and the second and third are the ones worth owning. Pain relief is what every competitor promises and what an insurance company pays for. Motion is what your patient actually wants and cannot get anywhere else in the sentence. It is also what your own stallion answer was about, which means it is already how you think.

In the brandWrite outcomes as activities, specifically and unglamorously: the third green, the grandchild on the shoulders, the trail. Photograph movement, never anatomy. Ask new patients what they stopped doing, and make that the record you measure against.

Your strategic sweet spot
The devoted surgeon.

Academic-grade hip and knee care, sold as a relationship instead of a procedure, priced in the open, and built so the promise survives.

The color thinking

You chose cobalt, crimson and white, and ruled out gold, marigold and ink. That combination is more interesting than it looks, because it removes both standard routes to “expensive”: no gold means no luxury shorthand, and no ink means no near-black to anchor the dark end. So cobalt has to do two jobs — it is both the brand colour and the deepest value in the system — which means owning a genuinely deep, saturated cobalt rather than the mid-blue every medical practice in the country already uses. White then becomes the actual luxury signal: unfilled space is the most expensive thing on a page, and it happens to argue the same point your business does. The one caution is crimson. In a surgical context, large fields of red read as blood or emergency, and your fifty-five-year-old is already nervous. Keep it to a single accent — a rule, a mark, one word — never a background, and never adjacent to clinical imagery. Used at one percent of the page it reads as vitality, which is what you meant by it. One further check to make early: cobalt, crimson and white together are the flag palette, and in a Tennessee practice that can read as patriotic before it reads as premium. Pushing the cobalt deeper than flag-blue and holding crimson to a genuine accent is most of what keeps the combination reading as yours rather than as a civic institution’s.

The type thinking

“Sturdy and characterful” is a useful brief because it rules out the two obvious wrong answers. It is not the thin geometric sans that every concierge practice defaults to, which reads generically premium and says nothing, and it is not institutional Helvetica-blue, which is the camp you are leaving. Sturdy plus characterful points to a serif with weight and a real voice for headlines — Fraunces at a low optical size carries warmth and heft without nostalgia; Instrument Serif is sharper and more editorial if Concept A leads. Set body copy in a grotesque with enough width to feel calm rather than efficient: Archivo or Mulish. Type sizes should run slightly larger than the category standard across the board. It is a small thing and it does more work than any other single decision here: your patients are over fifty, and a brand that is physically easy to read is making the same promise the practice is.

Our lean:Concept B, built on Concept A’s restraint. The private house gives you the register your style words asked for; the motion photography keeps it from becoming a bank. Concept C should survive inside both, in the portraiture — it is the right instinct in the wrong container.
08 · Summary

A Lover brand with a Caregiver’s architecture.

Devotion as the product, structure as the guarantee, and a model built deliberately enough that other surgeons should end up copying it. In a market selling throughput, prestige and performance, you are the only practice in a position to sell the relationship — and the only one that can put the price of it in writing.
