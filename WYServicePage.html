Two Paths to Compensation After a Crash

Florida is a no-fault state, which means your own insurance pays first no matter who caused the crash. That surprises most people, and it shapes everything that follows.

1
Personal Injury Protection Coverage

PIP pays a percentage of your medical bills and lost income up to your policy limit, regardless of fault. Every Florida driver is required to carry it. There is a deadline attached: you generally have to be seen by a provider within 14 days of the crash to qualify for those benefits.

PIP has real limits. It does not cover pain and suffering, and in a serious crash it rarely covers the medical bills either. How much is available depends on whether a provider documents an emergency medical condition.

2
A Claim Against the At-Fault Driver

Your injuries are serious enough or your losses exceed what PIP covers. At this point you can pursue a claim against the driver who caused the crash and their insurer.

That is where the full value of a case lives, because it is the only path that reaches pain and suffering and long-term losses.

Steps to Take After a Crash

What you do in the first days affects both your health and your claim.

  1. Call 911 and report the crash. Cooperate with the officer who writes the report.
  2. Exchange contact and insurance information with the other driver, and get names and numbers from anyone who stopped.
  3. Photograph the scene and the vehicles if you are able to.
  4. Get medical attention, even if you feel able to walk away. Some injuries do not show their full effect for days, and PIP has a 14-day treatment requirement.
  5. Be careful with the other driver’s insurer. An adjuster may call within a day or two asking for a recorded statement.

There is also a filing deadline. Most Florida negligence claims must be filed within two years of the date of the crash. That deadline was shortened from four years on March 24, 2023, and crashes before that date may still fall under the longer one. If you are unsure which applies to you, ask before you assume you are out of time.

Calculating What Your Claim Is Worth

There is no standard number. What a claim is worth depends on how badly you were hurt, how long you cannot work, whether you can return to the job you had, and how much insurance is available. Medical bills, missed paychecks, and vehicle damage are the straightforward part.

The harder part is what comes later. If your injuries mean you cannot do the work you did before, the loss is not only the weeks you missed. It is the difference between what you used to earn and what you can earn now, for as long as that lasts. In a serious case that is usually the largest number in the claim, and the one an insurer is slowest to account for.

Knowing what your claim is actually worth is what lets you recognize a low offer when you receive one.

Damages You May Be Able to Recover

Florida separates losses you can prove with bills and records from those that are harder to put a number on. A claim may include both.

Losses That Can Be Documented

  • Medical expenses, past and future
  • Lost income
  • Reduced ability to earn if you cannot return to the same work
  • Rehabilitation and physical therapy
  • Out-of-pocket expenses
  • Property damage

Losses That Are Harder to Measure

  • Pain and suffering
  • Emotional distress and anxiety
  • Post-traumatic stress
  • Reduced quality of life
  • Disfigurement and scarring
  • Loss of companionship and support for a spouse

What is available in your case depends heavily on which insurance coverages apply.

Being Blamed Does Not End Your Claim

Insurers often argue that the injured person was partly at fault. Under Florida law, being partly at fault usually reduces what you can recover rather than eliminating it. If you are found 20 percent at fault, the award is reduced by 20 percent.

As of March 24, 2023, a person found more than 50 percent at fault generally cannot recover from the other party, under Florida Statutes section 768.81. Before that date, there was no cutoff, and an injured person could recover a reduced amount no matter how much of the blame they carried. If your crash happened before March 2023, ask which version applies to you.

Either way, expect the argument. Shifting blame onto you is one of the cheapest ways for an insurer to reduce what it pays.

Before you pick up the phone

Dealing With the Insurance Adjuster

Insurance companies are businesses. They will go to some lengths to reduce what a claim costs them, and the person calling you is doing a job. Three precautions are worth knowing before you pick up.

Do not agree to a recorded statement

Even if the adjuster says it is required to process your claim. The questions are built to produce answers that can be used to reduce what you are paid.

Do not sign a blanket medical records release

They will ask for access to records connected to the crash. What they are often looking for is a pre-existing condition they can blame your injuries on.

Let your lawyer handle the communication

The adjuster is not working for you, and nothing you say to them is going to increase what they offer.

More Than One Party May Be Responsible

Who pays depends on how the crash happened. More than one party can share responsibility, and each may carry its own insurance policy.

  • The driver who caused the crash
  • A vehicle or parts manufacturer, if a defect contributed
  • A state or local agency, in the case of road design or a malfunctioning signal
  • A trucking company, if a commercial vehicle was involved
  • A rideshare company, if the other driver was working
  • An employer, if the other driver was on the job at the time

Identifying everyone who contributed matters for a practical reason. It can determine whether enough coverage exists to pay for your care and lost income.

Common Car Accident Injuries

Crashes produce everything from bruising to permanent disability. The injuries that drive the largest claims are usually these:

  • Traumatic brain injury and concussion
  • Spinal cord injury
  • Back and neck injuries, including whiplash
  • Chest injuries
  • Broken bones
  • Soft tissue injuries
  • Catastrophic and fatal injuries

Brain and spine injuries often do not show their full effect for days. Being seen early protects your health and creates the record of what the crash did.

Leading Causes of Car Crashes

Most crashes trace back to a short list of driver decisions.

  • Distracted driving, including texting
  • Speeding and following too closely
  • Driving under the influence
  • Fatigue or illness behind the wheel
  • Failure to yield the right of way
  • Defective vehicles and tires
  • Road defects and poor conditions

More than one cause often contributes to the same crash, which is part of why fault is worth investigating rather than assuming.

Car Accident Questions
Clients Ask Most

It’s Our Job To Provide Clarity
How much is my Tampa car accident case worth?

There is no standard amount. It depends on how serious your injuries are, how much medical care you need now and later, how much income you lost, whether you can return to your old work, and how much insurance is available.

How long do I have to file after a crash?

Most Florida negligence claims must be filed within two years of the crash. That deadline was shortened from four years on March 24, 2023, so earlier crashes may fall under the longer one. Waiting is costly well inside the deadline, because evidence does not stay available for long.

Can I still recover if they say I caused it?

Usually yes, unless you are found more than 50 percent at fault. Florida reduces recovery by your share of the blame, so 30 percent fault reduces the award by 30 percent. Above 50 percent, recovery is generally barred. That cutoff took effect March 24, 2023, so the date of your crash matters.

Who pays my medical bills after a crash?

Your own PIP coverage pays first, regardless of who caused the crash. It covers a percentage of medical bills and lost wages up to your policy limit, and you generally have 14 days to be seen. If your losses exceed what PIP covers, a claim against the at-fault driver may reach the rest.

Will my case go to trial?

Most do not. The majority of car accident claims resolve through negotiation, often without a lawsuit being filed. Cases are still prepared as though they will be tried, because that preparation is what gives a claim weight in negotiation.
