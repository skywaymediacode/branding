01 — Brand Colors

The Color Palette

Every concept draws from the same five-color coastal palette — deep Gulf water, tropical teal, palm lime, and a sunset orange accent. Whichever logo you choose, this palette carries through to the website, apparel, and signage for a consistent brand.

02 — Logo Concepts

Seven Directions to Choose From

The concepts range from classic athletic crests to playful mascots and clean modern marks. Each is shown with its strengths, considerations, and where it will work best.

1

The Championship Crest

Athletic

A varsity-style shield topped with a Florida pennant flag, featuring a leaping gamefish chasing a lure over a tropical shoreline, finished with a compass at the base. Structured, layered typography gives it a "summer camp meets sports franchise" feel.

StrengthsFeels official and established — like a camp with tradition. The pennant and compass add storytelling detail, and the stacked type reads clearly at most sizes.
ConsiderationsThe most detailed of the badge options; fine elements like the compass will simplify at very small sizes (favicon, embroidery).
Best forT-shirts, camp banners, letterhead, and anywhere a full "official crest" presence is wanted.
2

The Rope Badge

Nautical Classic

An oval badge wrapped in nautical rope, with a leaping fish over splashing surf, a sun silhouette of Florida behind it, and an anchor motif at the base. Bold banner typography for "FANTASY" gives it strong shelf presence.

StrengthsThe rope border and anchor lean into authentic maritime heritage. Excellent contrast and a strong, contained silhouette that holds up on patches and stickers.
ConsiderationsThe vertical oval format needs breathing room in wide layouts like website headers; a horizontal lockup companion would help.
Best forEmbroidered patches, hats, boat decals, and merchandise.
3

The Modern Mark

Clean & Minimal

A stripped-down, contemporary approach: a dynamic leaping fish rendered in flowing strokes, paired with a setting sun and simple wave lines. Clean sans-serif typography sits below in a balanced stacked lockup.

StrengthsThe most versatile and scalable of the seven — crisp at any size, works in one color, and feels current. The fish icon can stand alone as an app icon or watermark.
ConsiderationsLess "camp nostalgia" than the badge concepts; it reads more like a modern outfitter or guide service brand.
Best forWebsite, app, social avatars, email — digital-first branding.
4

The Mascot

Fun & Playful

A full-personality cartoon fish in a Florida trucker cap and shades, throwing a shaka and gripping a rod, bursting out of the water in front of the Skyway Bridge. Big, energetic hand-drawn lettering completes the look.

StrengthsInstantly memorable and kid-friendly — the strongest personality of the group. Perfect if the camp wants a character who can appear across marketing, signage, and social content.
ConsiderationsThe illustration is rich and detailed, so it needs size to shine; it's also the most casual direction, which may matter if adult charters are part of the audience.
Best forYouth camp marketing, event tees, social media, and mascot-driven campaigns.
5

The Monoline

Elegant Line Art

A refined single-weight line illustration of the Skyway Bridge arcing over stylized waves, with a leaping fish at center. Light, airy typography with generous letterspacing gives it a boutique, sophisticated character.

StrengthsThe most elegant and understated option — beautiful on premium applications like glassware, foil prints, and high-end apparel. The bridge is clearly the hero.
ConsiderationsThin line weights lose definition at small sizes and in low-contrast settings; it whispers where the other concepts shout.
Best forPremium merchandise, stationery, window decals, and upscale touchpoints.
6

The Retro Sunset

Vintage Florida

A richly illustrated vintage scene: a tarpon leaping from rolling surf beneath a blazing orange sunset, with the Skyway Bridge and a palm framing the composition. Distressed block letters and a hand-script "Fishing Camp" seal the retro feel.

StrengthsPure old-Florida nostalgia — feels like a classic surf-shop or tackle-brand graphic people want to wear. The sunset orange makes it the boldest, warmest concept of the set.
ConsiderationsAs a full illustrated scene it's better as a "hero graphic" than an everyday utility logo; pairing it with a simpler companion mark is recommended.
Best forSignature t-shirts, posters, stickers, and brand storytelling moments.
7

The Tampa Bay Seal

Local Landmark

A circular seal that puts the location front and center: the Skyway Bridge spans the full badge over a sunset, while a tarpon breaks the surface below. "Tampa Bay, FL" is proudly lettered at the base between twin palms.

StrengthsThe strongest sense of place in the lineup — unmistakably Tampa Bay. The circular format is timeless and works beautifully as a sticker, coin, or stamp.
ConsiderationsCurved text around a circle gets tight at small sizes; the full-scene interior carries a lot of detail for embroidery.
Best forStickers, boat and truck decals, signage, and location-proud merchandise.
03 — Making Your Choice

How to Decide

There's no wrong answer here — each concept is built on the same palette and can anchor the brand. A few questions to guide the conversation:

Who's the audience?

Youth-camp energy points to the Mascot (4) or Championship Crest (1). A broader all-ages audience favors the Rope Badge (2), Tampa Bay Seal (7), or Modern Mark (3).

Where will it live most?

Mostly digital? The Modern Mark (3) scales best. Mostly apparel and merch? The Retro Sunset (6) and Rope Badge (2) were made for fabric and stickers.

Consider a pairing

Many strong brands use two: a detailed hero graphic (6 or 1) for shirts and posters, plus a simple utility mark (3) for web, favicons, and embroidery.
