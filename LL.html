Using the gold

Three rules keep it classic.

A line, never a fill

Gold appears as a hairline: the rule beneath a headline, the frame around a photograph, the edge of a button. It never becomes a background or a block of colour. This is the single rule that separates gilding from gaudy.

One moment per view

A screen, a page or a post gets one gold element. The rule under the title, or the frame, or the divider — not all three. Two golds is decoration. One gold is a signature.

Metal, not yellow

On screen the gold is drawn as a gradient so it catches light the way real gilding does. For embroidery, print and anywhere a gradient cannot go, it flattens to a single warm #C6A45E and still reads as metal against the teal.

Imagery

Photographs sit inside the same hairline.

Frames overlap and step down the page rather than sitting in a tidy row — the same treatment as the original guide. Each keeps its two-pixel gold edge, and the overlap does the work: separate lives, touching.

Palette, connection, community, product — largest frame leads, each following frame steps down and behind.

Typography

One serif, three sans.

Fraunces is settled — everyone loves it, and it carries the warmth this brand needs. The open question is the sans beside it, which has to do two jobs at once: bold sentence-case subheads, and every word of body copy. Poppins was the request, and it also read a little masculine. So the architecture below stays fixed and only the sans changes. Three candidates, then one decision.

App icons

Gold leaf on a deepwater tile.

Each icon sits on a squircle of graded deepwater, edged with a hairline of the same gold foil used everywhere else in the system — real leaf, not a printed yellow. The glyphs stay Sea Mist at one stroke weight on a 24-pixel grid, and the single accent in each one is struck in foil. That is what keeps the set feeling made rather than assembled.

What we need

Two decisions and we build.

Confirm Plume, plus gold

The palette as shown — deepwater, harbor, lagoon, magenta, gilt and sea mist — with gold held to a line. If the gold wants to be warmer or paler, that is a five-minute change and worth saying now.

Pick the sans: A, B or C

One choice, and Fraunces stays either way. Everything downstream — app, site, social, print — is built on that answer, so it is worth ten minutes in the room rather than a reply later.

Where we land: B, Fraunces and Figtree. It answers the note about Poppins almost exactly — the same confident geometry with the edges warmed — and the softer letterforms let the Caregiver side of the brand sit underneath the Hero without weakening it. A stays the right answer if the team wants the bolder, more declarative voice.
