Ciper & Veil — Round 1: Seven marks

Seven monogram directions drawn from the archetype report: a Ruler at the core, an Everyman in the delivery, and the key and the pillars of an estate as the symbols chosen in the assessment. The marks are shown in black and white only, so the drawing is judged first. Type and colour follow in their own sections.

October 2026 · Prepared for Aveen Sufi

01

Marks

Each concept on white and reversed on ink. The recommendation is 06, The Operator: the two letters share a stem, which is the report’s own finding, Ruler authority and Everyman warmth in one letterform, and it holds its shape from a favicon to a trade-show wall.

01The Framework

Two fine arcs hold a single sharp stroke that runs clear of the circle. Structure first, then the decision inside it. The lightest drawing of the seven; it wants size and clear space.

02The Insider

One continuous stroke, C into V, signed off with a tail. A person’s hand on the work rather than a firm on the letterhead.

03The Perspective

Hairline ribbons laid down in sequence, each overlapping the last. The program being stood up, step by step. Finest of the set; needs a heavier cut below 40px.

04The Standard

A heavy high-contrast C running straight into the V’s thick diagonal, with the V’s right stroke curling back into a closed counter. Modern and sleek with one flourish; the boldest of the set at small sizes.

05The Estate

Inscribed capitals in stone proportions. The purest Ruler read, and the closest to outside counsel, so the voice has to carry the warmth.

06The Operator
Recommended

A high-contrast C whose bowl flows straight into the V’s heavy diagonal. Two archetypes, one stroke, no seam. Wide enough for a masthead, solid enough for an avatar.

07The Signature

The V’s descender turns back into a closed loop: the bow of a key, the symbol named in the assessment. Authority and access in one object.

Text & Typography

The brief asked for classical and high contrast. The archetype wants authority from the Ruler and plainness from the Everyman, so the roles are split: the wordmark and headlines in a high-contrast serif, everything operational in a plain humanist sans. Every face is shown on its own, without a mark, so the type is judged as type.

03

Colors

Five directions. Each keeps Ink #14161F and Sand #DCC7A0 from the brief and adds one depth colour, one paper and one muted tone. Nothing blue: cobalt is the compliance category’s colour, and stepping off it separates the brand from every platform dashboard on sight.

A · Ink & Sand

The brief, unchanged. Ink is the Ruler’s ground, sand is the Everyman’s warmth, white keeps it modern. Default for the website and social templates.

Text on light grounds uses Ink or the palette’s depth colour, never Sand. Sand on white fails contrast at any text size, so it stays a mark and surface colour.
Avoided, by the brief: Cobalt #2660B8, Violet #5B4A9E, Rose #D0697F. No foil, no marble, no gold gradient in the system; sand reads as light, not as gold.
Resemblance check: black is Deloitte’s ground and black-and-cream is old Big Law. Sand used generously and warmly is what separates the brand from both.
