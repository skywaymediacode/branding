Seaglass & USF Gold

Color · Two Palettes

The client's questionnaire selected three colors — Sand #EDEBD1, Lemongrass #DBE442, and Seaglass #80B0A6 — and a teal-into-green gradient. Sand has since been replaced by white #FFFFFF as the canvas, and USF Gold #CFC493, the University of South Florida's secondary color, joins the palette as the brand's warm color so that Project 10 reads as a USF-affiliated network. Lemongrass and Seaglass are honored in both palettes below. What changes from palette to palette is the deep working color that carries text, headings, and buttons (Seaglass itself measures only 2.3:1 on white and can never be text), and the supporting tint that sits between them.

USF Gold is used as a color, not a page background: cover bands, header blocks, panels, certificates. Anything set on it is held to the ratios below — body text on gold goes in the palette's Ink, and both working colors were deepened one step so they clear 4.5:1 on gold as well as on white. Palette 01 is the recommended system and is used throughout this document. Pick one; do not blend palettes across a single piece.

01Palette

Tidewater

Recommended

Pushes the working color toward true teal. Paired with USF Gold this reads as gold-and-teal on white — clearly related to the university, clearly not USF's own green-and-gold — and it is the palette that feels most "digital-first." Lemongrass pops harder against teal, so the five-percent cap matters even more here.

02Palette

Slate & Seaglass

Institutional

Answers the Ruler note in the assessment. A cool slate replaces the teal working color so Seaglass and Lemongrass become the only color in the system — white, USF Gold, and the slate neutrals do the structure; the client's colors do the personality. The more formal of the two; reads well on district and state-level documents.

Rules That Apply To Both

  • Seaglass #80B0A6 is never text. At 2.3:1 on white and 1.4:1 on USF Gold it fails every WCAG threshold, including large text. Use it as a field, a fill, or a rule.
  • Lemongrass is capped at about five percent of any composition: a background block, an icon fill, an active state, a single highlight. It is never a page background, a heading color, or body text.
  • The deep working color carries every word. All text under 24px is set in the palette's Primary or Ink. On USF Gold specifically, paragraphs are set in Ink; the Primary is reserved for headings, buttons, and links.
  • The gradient is one transition, one direction. Deep working color into Seaglass. Never three-plus hues, never into Lemongrass, never behind text.
  • Keep the teal cool and blue-cast. No warm, yellowed, or olive greens anywhere. Gold-and-teal is Project 10; gold-and-green is the university. The blue cast is what keeps the two related but distinct.
  • USF Gold is the only warm color. No orange, coral, or a second gold. Lemongrass is the only other warm-adjacent note, and it stays under five percent.
  • White is the canvas; USF Gold is a color. Every page and screen sits on white. Gold appears as a cover band, a header block, a panel, or a certificate ground — never as the background of a full text page, and never tinted or screened back.

Two Font Pairings

Typography

The brief asks for type that is geometric and character-driven at display size and open, tall-x-height, and legible at 14px in body — a PDF a teacher opens on her phone is the test case. Both pairings below meet that brief with free Google Fonts, and both use Fraunces italic as the single warm accent. The wordmark's "10" numeral is a drawn glyph and is not set in any of these faces.

Pairing 01 is the recommended system and is used throughout this document. Pairing 02 is its lighter, more Sage-forward alternative. Pick one pairing and one palette; do not mix across pieces.

01Pairing

Archivo + Mulish · Fraunces accent

Recommended

The report's own pairing. Archivo is a geometric grotesque with enough character to feel built rather than defaulted; Mulish has the open apertures and tall x-height that survive a 14px PDF opened on a phone. Fraunces italic is the single warm moment — the motto, a pull-quote — used once per page.

02Pairing

Instrument Sans + Nunito Sans

Lighter · Report alternate

The report's named alternates, paired together. Instrument Sans is narrower and quieter than Archivo, with a slight softness in the terminals; Nunito Sans is the same open, friendly text face family as Mulish with a touch more roundness. The more approachable of the two without tipping into Caregiver.
