Seaglass & Sand

Color · Five Palettes

The client's questionnaire selected three colors — Sand #EDEBD1, Lemongrass #DBE442, and Seaglass #80B0A6 — and a teal-into-green gradient. Those three are honored in every palette below. What changes from palette to palette is the deep working color that carries text, headings, and buttons (Seaglass itself measures only 2.01:1 on Sand and can never be text), and the supporting tint that sits between them.

Palette 01 is the recommended system and is used throughout this document. Palettes 02–05 are complete, accessibility-checked alternatives that shift the temperature or the emphasis while keeping the client's three chosen colors intact. Pick one; do not blend palettes across a single piece.

01Palette

Seaglass & Sand

Recommended

The report's own resolution of the client's three colors. Seaglass is deepened to a cool green-teal for ink and buttons; Sand becomes the canvas; Lemongrass is the single accent. Balanced, calm, and clearly on the education side of the line rather than the resort side.

05Palette

Tonal Seaglass

Monochrome

One hue, five steps. Everything is built from a single Seaglass scale running from near-white to near-black, with Sand as the warm counterpoint and Lemongrass as the only outside note. The quietest palette and the easiest to keep consistent across many hands — a good fit for a network that publishes a lot of documents from a lot of authors.

Rules That Apply To All Five

  • Seaglass #80B0A6 is never text. At 2.01:1 on Sand and 1.6:1 on white it fails every WCAG threshold, including large text. Use it as a field, a fill, or a rule.
  • Lemongrass is capped at about five percent of any composition: a background block, an icon fill, an active state, a single highlight. It is never a page background, a heading color, or body text.
  • The deep working color carries every word. Whichever palette is chosen, all text under 24px is set in that palette's Primary or Ink.
  • The gradient is one transition, one direction. Deep working color into Seaglass, or the tonal scale in Palette 05. Never three-plus hues, never into Lemongrass, never behind text.
  • Keep greens cool and grey-cast. No warm, yellowed, or olive greens anywhere — they pull the brand toward USF's green-and-gold and toward "eco" clichés.
  • Never pair with gold, orange, or coral. Warm accents plus green is the university's territory; Lemongrass is the only warm-adjacent note permitted.
  • Sand is the canvas, white is the exception. Use pure white only for cards, forms, and images that need a clean edge inside a Sand page.

Five Font Pairings

Typography

The brief asks for type that is geometric and character-driven at display size and open, tall-x-height, and legible at 14px in body — a PDF a teacher opens on her phone is the test case. All five pairings below meet that brief with free Google Fonts, and all five use Fraunces italic as the single warm accent (in Pairing 05 it steps up to the display role). The wordmark's "10" numeral is a drawn glyph and is not set in any of these faces.

Pairing 01 is the recommended system and is used throughout this document. Pairings 02–05 shift the balance between the Sage and Creator halves of the archetype. Pick one pairing and one palette; do not mix across pieces.

01Pairing

Archivo + Mulish · Fraunces accent

Recommended

The report's own pairing. Archivo is a geometric grotesque with enough character to feel built rather than defaulted; Mulish has the open apertures and tall x-height that survive a 14px PDF opened on a phone. Fraunces italic is the single warm moment — the motto, a pull-quote — used once per page.

The report's named alternates, paired together. The most approachable of the five without tipping into Caregiver.

03Pairing

Space Grotesk + Work Sans

Most built · Creator-forward

The most overtly geometric option. Space Grotesk has visibly constructed letterforms — the single-storey a, the squared-off counters — that make headings feel engineered. Work Sans is a neutral, highly legible text face that stays out of the way. Together they lean hardest into "unmistakably built."

04Pairing

Bricolage Grotesque + Figtree

Most character

The pairing with the most personality in the display face. Bricolage Grotesque has an optical-size axis: at headline sizes it tightens and gains quirks — a curled t, a distinctive g — that carry the client's "playful, whimsical" preference in type without touching the copy. Figtree is a clean, friendly geometric text face that keeps everything else composed.

05Pairing

Fraunces + Mulish

Serif-led · Sage-forward

The one serif-led option. Fraunces moves from accent to display: a soft, slightly old-style serif with an optical-size axis that keeps it crisp at headline sizes and warm at text sizes. Mulish carries the body exactly as in Pairing 01. This is the most "knowing" of the five — a wise voice, a cultivated feel — at the cost of some of the geometric "built" quality.
