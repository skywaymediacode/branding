Brand style guide · Version 1.0 · November 2026

A Ruler’s command. An Everyman’s footing.

How Cipher & Veil looks, sounds and behaves, built from the archetype assessment and the logo rounds that followed. It is written for anyone who makes something with the brand on it: the website, a deck, a LinkedIn post, a trade-show wall. Where it gives a rule, the rule exists so the brand reads the same way everywhere. Where it gives a reason, the reason is so you can make the next decision without asking.

Prepared for Aveen Sufi · Skyway Media

01

The brand in one page

Cipher & Veil builds AI governance, data privacy and third-party risk programs for small and mid-size companies that have found themselves regulated: forty to four hundred people, in healthcare, fintech and any industry the rules reached first. The product is not advice about governance. It is the program itself, built by someone who has built it before.

Tagline

A different perspective.
Earned from the inside.

Carried on every surface where a competitor would put a credential. Never shortened, never restyled as a hashtag, never set in caps.

Purpose

Give the companies nobody built this for a program that actually runs.

Order for the people who need it, delivered from the inside, in plain words, as a colleague. If a piece of work does not end with the client knowing what to do on Monday, it is not finished.

Archetype · core

Ruler

Decisive, ordered, in command. The deliverable is structure that holds under pressure and a clear answer to “what do we do.” The Lion is the ambassador; the pillars of an estate are the setting.

Archetype · delivery

Everyman

Plain, present, one of us. The operator in the room rather than the firm on the letterhead. “We are operators, not just advisors” is the vantage point, and the voice should sound like it.

Notes, not drivers

Magician · Lover · Jester

Transformation (“anything can happen”), warmth and craft in the delivery, and a dry wit about an industry that takes itself seriously. Each colours the two that lead. None of them leads.

Pillar 1

Built, not briefed.

Every credential line becomes a built line. “Built the privacy program at a regulated healthcare company. Now builds yours.” Case stories are written as what we stood up, never what we advised on.

Pillar 2

Say what to do on Monday.

Every piece of content ends in an instruction. Sequences, not frameworks. “Here’s the order of operations” replaces “it depends.” Name the sequences and reuse them.

Pillar 3

The operator in the room.

“We” and “I,” never “the firm.” Aveen’s name and face on the site and the templates. One opinion per post. Candour in the voice, courtesy in the manners.

Colour

Evergreen & Stone. Ink is the Ruler’s ground; sand is the Everyman’s warmth, the ground you stand on rather than the gold you are paid in; evergreen is the estate, established and quietly expensive without borrowing the legal category’s navy. Nothing blue, ever: cobalt belongs to the compliance platforms, and staying off it is half the differentiation.

Text on light grounds is ink or evergreen, never sand or stone. Both fail against paper and white at any text size.

04

Typography

One family does the work: Bricolage Grotesque, for the wordmark, headlines and body. It is a grotesk with an editorial voice, and its optical-size axis means the headline cut and the text cut are drawn differently while staying one face. Bricolage has no italic, so emphasis uses Instrument Serif italic, a long-standing editorial convention that also keeps the serif-and-sans split visible inside a paragraph. The Fraunces italic ampersand appears in the wordmark only.

Headlines are sentence case, never all caps, and never longer than two lines at display size. Tighten tracking slightly as size goes up.
Body sits at 16–18px, 60–70 characters a line, left-aligned, ragged right. Numbered sequences are set as real numbered lists; they are the brand’s signature content.
Emphasis is Instrument Serif italic, one phrase at a time. No faux italic, no underlines, and bold only for UI and labels.
05

Voice

Decide like a Ruler. Speak like the person who ran the program. If the brand were a person, she is the legal technologist who wrote the privacy program at a regulated company, got the AI policy past the board, and now walks into a forty-person fintech and says what to do first, second and third. Calm, direct, a little dry about the industry’s ceremony. She never says “it depends” without immediately saying what it depends on.

Decisive

We are clear, directive, sequenced. Not bureaucratic, deck-speak, or “considerations for leadership.”
Here’s the order of operations. Vendor inventory first, policy second, training third. Don’t start with the policy.

Down-to-earth

We are plain-spoken, practical, in the room. Not folksy, apologetic, or vague about what we actually do.
You don’t need a framework. You need a vendor list, an owner for it, and a question you ask every new vendor.

Candid

We are sharp, honest about the industry, willing to say the quiet part. Not cynical, snide, or anti-lawyer.
A sixty-page assessment is not a program. It’s a description of the program you still don’t have.

Earned

We are specific about what we’ve built, confident without proving it. Not credential-stacking, name-dropping, or humble-bragging.
We built this at a company that gets audited. Then we built it again at one that didn’t, yet.
The dials
directive over consultativeplain over polishedcandid over diplomaticoperator over expertdry over earnestsequence over frameworkwarm, but never soft on what has to be done

The guardrail. Authority comes from having done the job, never from the letterhead or the volume. If a line needs a credential to land, cut the credential and name the thing you built instead. If a line would make a general counsel wince, rewrite it; if it would make them nod, keep it.

Website hero

“AI governance, data privacy and third-party risk, built by the people who built them before. A different perspective. Earned from the inside.”

LinkedIn post

“Most companies don’t have an AI governance problem. They have an ownership problem. Nobody owns the vendor list. Fix that, and half the policy writes itself.”

Trade-show opener

“I’m not going to assess your program. I’m going to tell you the three things it’s missing, and then we’ll build them.”

Pronouns. “We” and “I.” Never “the firm,” “our practice” or “Cipher & Veil” referring to itself in the third person.
Words we use: program, build, stand up, owner, sequence, order of operations, on Monday. Words we don’t: framework, considerations, leverage, best-in-class, holistic, journey, trusted partner.
Every piece ends in an instruction. A post, a page, a deck: the last line tells the reader what to do first.
06

Imagery & graphic language

Two kinds of picture, and no stock. The lion, painted, for the brand’s own moments; and documentary photography of the actual work for everything else. The graphic language is the program itself: numbered sequences, real documents, checklists set like typography.

The lion

Level head. Four paws on open ground.

The brand’s one image, commissioned for the report. Painted in the palette: ink for the ground, sand for the light, white flecks in the underpainting and never on the lion. Use it on the About page, the booth wall and the cover of the capabilities deck. Never crop to the face alone, never as a watermark, never as an icon.

Photography

The work, not the handshake.

Documentary photographs of the real thing: a vendor questionnaire on a desk, a policy marked up in pen, a whiteboard with the order of operations on it, Aveen in a client’s room. Natural light, no retouching beyond colour, people shown working rather than posing. No boardroom stock, no abstract “cyber” imagery, no padlocks.

Graphic devices

Numbers, rules, sequences.

Large numerals in Bricolage for steps and sections. Hairline rules in stone or sand to separate, never boxes with shadows. The mark used large and cropped as a pattern on dark surfaces, in sand at low opacity. The key and the pillars are references in the writing, not drawn symbols.

08

Fonts, hosting & privacy

Every font on every Cipher & Veil property is served from the brand’s own domain. No request goes to Google Fonts, Adobe Fonts or any third-party font service, so no visitor IP leaves the site to load type. All four faces are open-licence and may be self-hosted without fee.

Font files on the server. WOFF2 for Bricolage Grotesque (variable), Instrument Serif Italic, Fraunces Italic (subset to the ampersand) and Geist 500–600, declared with @font-face and preloaded.
Theme and builder audit. Any Google Fonts enqueue disabled; builder “load Google Fonts” off; every text style pointed at the self-hosted families.
Plugin sweep. Every active plugin checked for font, icon-font or embed requests to third-party hosts, and replaced or disabled where it does.
Network proof. A full-site crawl of outgoing requests before launch confirms zero calls to font hosts; the report is kept as evidence.
Documents and decks. Fonts installed locally for anyone producing brand material; PDFs exported with fonts embedded.
Privacy page. States that fonts are self-hosted. A small, true thing to be able to say for a governance brand.
Asset folder
  • Logo: horizontal lockup in SVG, PDF and PNG (white, evergreen, paper, ink, sand, one-colour); mark alone in standard, medium, small and square cuts; wordmark alone; favicon set (16, 32, 180, 512).
  • Colour: ASE/GPL swatch files and the hex/RGB values above.
  • Type: WOFF2 files and the CSS @font-face block; a Figma/Illustrator text-style set matching the scale.
