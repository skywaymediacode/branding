01 Brand Foundation

What Aquability
stands for.

Aquability is not a general accessibility contractor. It does one thing, which is getting people back into the water, and it owns the entire path there, including the concrete. Every decision in this guide protects that focus.

North star

Back in the water.

The internal rallying line, not a public tagline. It is the sentence every piece of work should be measured against. If a page, a photo or a paragraph doesn't move someone closer to the water, it's decoration.

Positioning

For Florida homeowners and pool operators who need accessible entry to a pool, Aquability is the dedicated pool lift specialist that handles selection, deck assessment, concrete and certified installation end to end, so access does not depend on coordinating three different trades.

Promise

Chosen correctly. Anchored properly. Installed once.

Three clauses, three real risks removed: the wrong model, an unsafe anchor, and a job that has to be redone. This is the most usable line in the whole system. It works as a homepage subhead, a proposal header and a truck decal.

Essence

Considered access. Access that has been thought about properly. The deck, the counterweight, the transfer height and the person, rather than a piece of equipment dropped beside a pool.

What we are not

Not a medical supply catalogue. Not a general handyman. Not a discount installer. Not a charity. Aquability sells a considered outcome at a fair price, to people who are making a significant decision about their home or their facility.

1.1

Personality

Four traits. Each is paired with the trait it must not tip into. That boundary is where most brands lose their voice.

01

We deal in specifications, measurements and timelines. Model numbers, anchor depths, cure times. Precision is how trust is earned in a trade where mistakes are physical.

Not pedantic
02

Behind every enquiry is a person whose relationship with their own pool has changed. We speak to the human situation first and the hardware second.

Not sentimental
03

No countdowns, no scarcity, no pressure. This is a considered purchase and the brand behaves like it. Space in the layout is the visual form of the same idea.

Not passive
04
Straight

Plain answers, including the unwelcome ones: “your deck needs concrete first”, “a portable won't hold on that surface”. Saying no is part of the service.

Not blunt
1.2

Who we are speaking to

Primary

Homeowners in transition

Families adapting to a change in mobility: ageing in place, post-surgical rehabilitation, a progressive condition. The pool is already there; what's changed is the ability to get into it.

Note: the enquiry is very often made by an adult child or a spouse, not the person who will use the lift. Copy must speak comfortably to both.

Secondary

Commercial operators

HOAs, condominium boards, hotels, rehabilitation clinics and municipal aquatic centres. Here the trigger is usually compliance and liability rather than a personal need.

Note: this audience wants specification, lead time and paperwork. Serve them with detail, not emotion.

Influencers

The referral network

Physical and occupational therapists, case managers, pool builders and general contractors. They rarely buy, but they recommend repeatedly, to exactly the right people.

Note: give them something to hand over. A clear, printable one-pager beats a brochure.

02 Messaging Framework

Four things worth
saying repeatedly.

These pillars are the argument for Aquability. Every page of the site should carry at least one; the homepage should carry all four.

Pillar 01

One team, start to finish

Selection, deck assessment, concrete, installation and service, all under one roof and one point of accountability. The alternative is a homeowner project-managing a supplier, a concrete crew and an installer who have never met.

Says it in a line: “One number to call, from the first question to the final walkthrough.”

Pillar 02

Specialists, not a catalogue

Pool lifts are the whole business. That focus is the reason the recommendation is right the first time. As an authorised Aqua Creek dealer we fit the model to the pool, the deck and the person, rather than to whatever is in stock.

Says it in a line: “Pool lifts are all we do.”

Pillar 03

Anchored properly

A pool lift is a counterweighted machine that lifts a person over water. It has to be anchored into concrete to be safe. We assess whether the deck is pavers over concrete or needs a new pour, and if it does, we pour it.

Says it in a line: “We check what's under your deck before we quote what goes on top of it.”

Pillar 04

A calm, guided process

Free consultation, deck assessment, recommendation, installation, walkthrough. Five steps, stated up front. Naming the process removes the anxiety of an unfamiliar purchase, and it is itself a differentiator.

Says it in a line: “You'll know every step before we start.”

02 Installation

We check what's under your deck.

A pool lift is a counterweighted machine. It has to be anchored into concrete to be safe, which means the first question is not which lift you want, it is what your deck is made of.

Most Florida pool decks are pavers laid over a concrete base, and in that case the anchor goes straight in. Where there's no slab underneath, new concrete has to be poured and given time to cure before anything is fixed to it. We assess this at the consultation so the quote you get is the price you pay.

Book a free consultation See lift models

  • Set headlines in Newsreader 400 and let size create the hierarchy.
  • Tighten tracking as headlines get larger (−2% to −3.5%).
  • Cap measure at 68 characters, always.

06 Voice & Tone

Say it the way you'd
say it at the pool deck.

Aquability sells to people at a vulnerable moment, often a family adjusting to something they did not plan for. The voice earns trust by being plain, specific and completely free of pressure. Marketing language is the fastest way to lose this audience.

6.1

Four principles

Principle 01

Lead with the person

The subject of the sentence is a swimmer, not a lift. “You'll be able to get in and out on your own” beats “the ADA-compliant PALS lift features a 400 lb capacity”. Specifications follow the benefit; they don't replace it.

Principle 02

Numbers beat adjectives

“Most installs take a day” is worth more than “fast, professional installation”. Where a real number exists, whether that is a capacity, a lead time, a cure time or a warranty length, use it. Where one doesn't, say so plainly rather than reaching for a superlative.

Principle 03

Answer before selling

Most visitors arrive with one of four questions: will it work at my pool, what does it cost, how long does it take, is it safe. Answer those as directly as the facts allow, then offer the consultation. A page that withholds information to force a phone call reads as a trap.

Principle 04

Never manufacture urgency

No countdowns, no “limited time”, no exclamation marks, no capitalised CALL NOW. The decision is a considered one and the brand's calm is a competitive advantage. Urgency tactics signal a company that needs the sale more than the customer needs the lift.

6.2

Tone shifts by context

The voice is constant; the tone moves along one axis, from reassuring to technical, depending on who is reading and what they need.

Reassuring & human Precise & technical
6.3

Language rules

We sayWe don't sayWhy
Pool lift Handicap chair, disabled lift The industry term, and the neutral one. It describes the equipment, not the person.
A swimmer who uses a wheelchair Wheelchair-bound, confined to a wheelchair Person first. “Bound” is both inaccurate and loaded. A wheelchair enables movement, it does not restrict it.
Accessible pool entry Special needs access Accessibility is a standard, not a special case. Framing it as special makes the customer the exception.

Banned words
  • solutions, seamless, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class
  • revolutionise, unlock, elevate, empower, game-changing
  • handicapped, the disabled, suffering from, victim of
  • “simply” and “just”, which dismiss something that is genuinely difficult
  • Exclamation marks. Anywhere. Including form confirmations.
Mechanics
  • Sentence case for all headlines and buttons. Never Title Case.
  • Second person: “your pool”, “you'll”. Contractions are fine.
  • “We” for the company; never “Aquability believes” in the third person.
  • Serial comma. Numerals for anything measurable (4 hours, 400 lb, 2 in).
  • One idea per paragraph. Three sentences is usually enough.

07 Art Direction

Photograph the swim,
not the difficulty.

Accessibility marketing has a default visual language: a person looking wistfully at water, shot from above, in soft focus. It is pitying and it is everywhere. Aquability's imagery should look like architectural and product photography: eye level, clean light, real installations.

7.1

Six principles

01

Water at eye level

Shoot from deck height or lower. Looking down at someone is the single most common mistake in this category and it changes the entire meaning of the picture.

02

Hands and hardware

Close detail on the anchor plate, the seat, the control paddle, a hand on the armrest. These shots do the work of proving craft, and they carry no risk of looking staged.

03

Real installs, real light

Actual Florida jobs in natural light. No studio stock, no models by a rendered pool. If a stock image is unavoidable, use hardware detail rather than people.

04

In motion, not posed

The subject is entering, swimming or talking to family, always mid-action. Nobody looks at the camera. Nobody is framed alone at the edge of the water.

05

Give it room

Wide crops with generous negative space: deck, water, sky. The composure of the layout should carry into the photography. Crowded frames read as cheap.

06

Cool, honest grade

Hold the blues, keep whites clean, let concrete stay warm-neutral. No heavy filters, no orange-and-teal, no vignettes. The palette is already in the water.

7.2

Framing references

Compositional guides for the photographer. Not final imagery, and not to be used as artwork.

Water at eye level

Camera on the deck, horizon low, subject in the water.

Hands & hardware

Tight on the anchor, the seat, the control. Proof of craft.

The deck, in context

Wide, calm, architectural. The lift as part of the property.

Motion, not portrait

Swimming, moving, mid-conversation. Never posed at the edge.

Do
  • Shoot at or below the subject's eye line.
  • Include family, carers and staff. Access is social.
  • Photograph completed installations on real properties.
  • Keep the lift in frame but rarely as the hero.
  • Leave clean, uncluttered areas for headline overlays.
Don't
  • Shoot down at a wheelchair user from standing height.
  • Use stock imagery of models pretending to need a lift.
  • Isolate a single person at the water's edge looking away.
  • Show the equipment without any human context at all.
  • Apply warm filters, heavy vignettes or blue-orange grading.

Decisive entry,
gentle settle.

Motion behaves like water finding its level. It moves quickly at first and eases into place. It is never bouncy, never playful, and never applied to more than one thing at a time.

Principles
  • Reveal on scroll once, never on scroll-up. Repeated animation is exhausting.

  • Fade and translate together. Never scale, rotate, or bounce.
  • Nothing autoplays: no carousels, no looping video, no parallax.

  • If motion is the most interesting thing on a page, the page has a content problem.

03 Before we quote

We check what is under your deck.

A pool lift is a counterweighted machine, so it has to be anchored into concrete to be safe. That makes your deck the first question rather than the last one.

Most Florida pool decks are pavers laid over a concrete base, and in that case the anchor goes straight into what is already there. Where there is no slab underneath, new concrete has to be poured and given time to cure before anything is fixed to it. We establish which situation you are in at the consultation, so the number you are quoted is the number you pay.

Book a deck assessment
What we are looking for
Pavers over concrete
The anchor goes straight into the existing base. Pavers are lifted, the anchor is set, the pavers go back.
Existing concrete slab
Assessed for depth and condition against the manufacturer's anchoring requirement for the model.
Pavers over sand or shell
There is nothing to hold the counterweight. A new pour is required before the lift can be installed.
No deck at the entry point
We design and pour a pad sized to the lift and to the way the pool is actually entered.
