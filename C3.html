How to read this

Already settled

  • The wordmark. Fraunces 600, fully outlined — no font dependency, and it cannot be accidentally re-typeset.
  • The tagline. “Elevating People,” Inter, letterspaced, always subordinate.
  • The stroke system. Same weight, same round cap, same optical size, so the marks are genuinely comparable. Nucleus sets its numeral in Jost 600. Cipher is the exception — that is your supplied artwork, placed unaltered.
  • The one-accent rule. Gold touches exactly one element. Never the wordmark.

Being decided here

  • Which idea the mark carries — the ripple, the ascent, the C3 fused into one letterform, or the name held at the centre.
  • Which palette that idea lives in — and whether C3 stays in the blue category or steps out of it.

Every mark shown is a working file, not a sketch. Whichever pair you choose ships the same day in horizontal, stacked, mark-only and app-icon form, on dark, on light, and in one colour.

RippleA

Three concentric open arcs

The human story — “purpose is multiplied through people,” drawn literally. Three C’s and a ripple radiating outward in one form.

Where it wins

Most meaning per stroke. The only mark that visualises the ripple effect the model is built on. Already built and delivered with the homepage.

What it costs

Busiest of the five. The inner arc closes up below about 24px, so the favicon needs a reduced two-arc cut.

Best used for

Web, print, motion — anywhere it can live above 32px.

AltitudeB

Three ascending chevrons

The heritage. Air Force, altitude, “Elevating People” — rank insignia read as upward movement.

Where it wins

Instantly legible at any size and unmistakably about ascent. Strong with first-responder and military audiences.

What it costs

Chevrons carry rank and hierarchy. In a clinical or education room that can read as command-and-control rather than partnership.

Best used for

First-responder, defense and public-safety facing material.

InterlockC

Two open C’s, linked

One practice, two doors, drawn as one form. Two C’s that only mean anything together — the organisation and the person leading it, held in the same shape.

Where it wins

The only mark here that says collaborate rather than just C3. Reads as partnership rather than authority, and the open gaps keep it from feeling like a closed system. Still readable at 16px in one colour.

What it costs

Linked rings are well travelled — chain links, infinity marks, payment logos. It leans on the two-colour split to stay distinct; in one colour it reads more as a monogram than a link.

Best used for

Partnership and org-facing material, co-branded work, contracts — anywhere the collaboration is the point.

05

CipherD

The supplied C3 mark, used as drawn

The C and the 3 fused into one letterform, drawn with mitred terminals rather than round caps — sharper, more editorial, closer to a typeset numeral than a drawn stroke.

Where it wins

Final artwork placed unaltered: nothing here is an interpretation. The flat cuts give it more edge than anything else in the set, and the taller numeral makes it the most confident of the five at large sizes.

What it costs

It is the one mark that breaks the family’s round-cap stroke logic, so it does not sit inside a system with the others — it asks to be the whole identity rather than one option among five. It is also a single colour, which puts it outside the one-accent rule.

Best used for

Large format — covers, signage, building graphics, the top of a proposal.

06

NucleusE

Two concentric arcs holding a 3

The ripple with the name inside it. Arcs radiating out from a 3 held at the centre — the practice built around the person, and the person at the centre of the practice.

Where it wins

Says C3 outright and keeps the ripple’s idea. Where Cipher fuses the letter and the numeral into one form, this leaves them legible as separate parts — easier to read at a glance, easier to explain in a room.

What it costs

The most elements of any mark here — two arcs plus a two-stroke numeral. The 3 fills in below about 32px, so the favicon needs the reduced cut.

Best used for

Primary lockup, letterhead, signage and decks — anywhere it lives above 32px and the name has to be unmistakable.
